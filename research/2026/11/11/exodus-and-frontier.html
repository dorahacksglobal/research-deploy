The Exodus and The Frontier

11 Nov 2026

Eric Zhang

An Exodus to America is a flight from the persecution found everywhere else on Earth, mainly totalitarian governments and the suppression of freedom. America has been the frontier of human civilization in freedom, and so, often, in ideas and technology. In this Exodus, America is the promised land.

Bitcoin is an Exodus technology. People fleeing worldly persecution frequently leave their wealth behind: accounts are frozen, property is seized, and capital controls close the borders. Bitcoin gives the persecuted an instrument to convert their physical assets into intangible, digital gold that crosses any border.

Yet there is another Exodus, recently best revealed by Peter Thiel in his Nashville Antichrist lectures. During a lecture in September, he said he had sold his bitcoin when he realized that it is too easy to trace. Whether he actually did so is not important. If we interpret him, what he really meant was a second kind of Exodus: the Exodus from America.

An Exodus from America is an exit from a different kind of bondage: over-regulation, the threat of political persecution, and an environment possibly hostile to builders in the future. Mark 10:30 reveals why this bondage is unavoidable: “But he shall receive an hundredfold now in this time, houses, and brethren, and sisters, and mothers, and children, and lands, with persecutions;” Worldly wealth on that scale cannot come without persecution, and persecution always brings bondage with it.

This Exodus leads to the Frontier. A frontier is the edge where the rules have not yet been written, and where results depend on what a person can build rather than whom they can persuade. America once offered such an edge on its own soil, until the continent was settled and the 1890 census declared the frontier line closed. Since then the Frontier has moved: outward into the physical universe, and forward into miracle-like technologies, built at speed. Both demand enormous capital, tolerance for risk, and freedom to act before permission arrives. They are therefore where a permission-based regulatory state does the most damage. The Exodus from America is thus not an exit from American soil, which is where much of this building still happens, but from the American establishment toward the place where America’s founding promise now lives. The first Exodus is a flight for survival; the second is a departure for the sake of the work.

Why is the Frontier a promised land? Like Abraham’s, it is a land not yet seen: “Get thee out of thy country… unto a land that I will shew thee” (Genesis 12:1), and he “went out, not knowing whither he went” (Hebrews 11:8). It is also where the oldest mandate to builders is still unfulfilled: “replenish the earth, and subdue it” (Genesis 1:28). This land is not handed over; it has to be made. So there are two types of Exodus in the world today, each with its own promised land. In the Exodus to America, America is the promised land. In the Exodus from America, the Frontier itself is.

Bitcoin is still extremely effective for the Exodus to America, but not so much for the Exodus from America. It answers one kind of bondage, the seizure of wealth. Against a rogue regime that confiscates, it works. The builder’s bondage is a different kind. A founder blocked by a license, a regulatory haunt, or a tax cannot escape by moving money, and a rocket or a laboratory cannot be held in a seed phrase. Worse, the American state has strong chain analysis and reaches the exchanges through compliance, so against it Bitcoin’s traceability is a weakness. This is Thiel’s point. On both the geographical and the technological frontier, Bitcoin is therefore not the decisive instrument. Its role is at most supporting and marginal: it helps when persecution takes the form of debanking or frozen accounts, but that is not what the Frontier is about.

That doesn’t mean Bitcoin has no value. Many people living under capital controls or the risk of confiscation still need it, and the first Exodus will always exist. Bitcoin will save many lives and preserve a vast number of talents from persecution in the time to come.
